What it is

A vault built for client work, not just passwords

Agencies and freelancers juggle dozens of client accounts: Workspace, Firebase, cloud and API keys, GitHub, Apple Developer, Play Console, hosting, banks. Today they live in spreadsheets and chat threads. Keyvane gives them a proper home.

Organised by client

Clients, then projects, then environments. Fifteen templates for the accounts you actually use, from Firebase and Play Console to .env files and Wi-Fi.

Only you hold the keys

Encrypted on your device before anything is saved. Storage providers, and TechHigh, only ever receive scrambled bytes.

Your storage, your choice

Pick where the vault file lives and move it any time. Works offline; the cloud is just a mirror.

How it works

Zero knowledge, in plain words

"Zero knowledge" means nobody but you can read your vault — not the company that made the app, not the cloud that stores it. Here is how Keyvane gets there.

  1. You choose a master password

    It stays in your head. It is never sent anywhere and never written to disk.

  2. Your device makes a Secret Key

    A long random code, kept in your device's keychain and printed on your Emergency Kit. It never goes in the vault file and is never uploaded.

  3. Both are turned into an encryption key

    Argon2id combines them. Because the Secret Key adds 160 bits of randomness, a stolen vault file cannot be guessed, even if your password is weak.

  4. The vault is sealed before it leaves

    Everything is encrypted with XChaCha20-Poly1305 on your device. Only then is the file saved to your storage.

The honest trade-off. If you lose both your master password and your Emergency Kit, nobody can recover your vault. Not TechHigh, not your storage provider. That is the point — and it is why the app insists you print the kit and re-checks your password every 14 days so you do not forget it.

Full details, including the file format and threat model, are on the Security page.

Features

Made for the questions clients actually ask

"Who owns this account?" "Whose phone has the 2FA?" "When does the certificate expire?" "Can you hand everything over?" Keyvane answers them.

Clients → projects → environments

Group every credential by client, project and environment (production, staging, development). Tags, favourites and search across everything.

Account ownership and 2FA holder records

Mark each account as owned by you, the client or the agency. Record the 2FA method, who holds it and where the backup codes are. No more "ask Sarah".

Built-in 2FA codes

Generate time-based codes in the vault, scan QR codes, import from Google Authenticator. Keep the secret with the login it belongs to.

Expiry reminders

Developer memberships, signing certificates, API keys, domains: set an expiry date and get reminded before it bites.

Handover pack

When a project ends, export an encrypted pack for the client with just their accounts, plus a rotate-and-revoke checklist. History never travels with it.

Developer tools

Password, passphrase and API-key generator. Copy or export .env files. Attach certificates, keystores and service-account JSON. The kv command line and an SSH agent.

Teams and agencies

Organisations: share with people, not with the server

Companies keep shared credentials in collections, usually one per client, and decide exactly who can open each one. Each person's personal vault stays private and is never visible to the organisation.

Collections and roles

Owner, admin, manager, member and guest roles. Per-collection permissions: autofill only, view, edit or manage. Guests get an end date and lose access automatically.

Verified invites

New members read five verification words to an admin over a call before they receive any keys. That is what stops anyone, including the server, slipping in their own key.

Offboarding with key rotation

Remove a person in one step. Keyvane replaces the key of every collection they could open and lists the passwords they saw, so you can change them at each service.

Encrypted activity log and policies

Who viewed, copied, autofilled, edited or shared — encrypted so only owners and admins can read it. Set password strength, biometric and auto-lock policies.

Keyvane Cloud, or your own server, stores only encrypted files, public keys and the organisation's structure. See what the server can and cannot see on the Privacy Policy and Security page.

No lock-in

An open format you can leave with

Your vault is a single documented .kvault file: JSON on the outside, encrypted on the inside. The format and the encryption are specified so that anyone can verify them or write their own reader. If Keyvane disappeared tomorrow, your data would still open.

An external security audit and the publication of the core library as open source are on the roadmap before public launch.

FAQ

Questions people ask

What happens if I forget my master password?

Use the master password written on your Emergency Kit. If you have lost both the password and the kit, the vault cannot be opened by anyone, including TechHigh. There is no reset link, because a reset link would mean someone else could open your vault too.

What is the Secret Key, and do I have to type it every time?

It is a long random code your device generates at sign-up. It is kept in your device's secure keychain, so you only type it when you set up a new device or restore from a backup. Keep the printed copy on your Emergency Kit somewhere safe.

Can TechHigh see my passwords if I use Keyvane Cloud?

No. Keyvane Cloud receives the same encrypted file as any other storage option. It also stores your email address, organisation and group names, and who can open which collection, so that sharing works. It never receives your master password, Secret Key, vault contents or collection names. The full list is in the Privacy Policy.

Does it work offline?

Yes. The vault lives on your device and opens, edits and autofills without a connection. Changes sync and merge when you are back online.

Can my client see what I keep in Keyvane?

Only what you choose to share. In an organisation, each collection has its own key and its own access list. Your personal vault is never visible to the organisation. For clients without Keyvane, the handover pack gives them an encrypted file with just their accounts.

Why not just use a general password manager?

You can, and many do. Keyvane adds the structure client work needs — clients, projects, environments, account ownership, who holds the 2FA, expiry dates, handover — and lets you keep the data in storage you already control rather than on a vendor's servers.

Is the code open source?

The file format and encryption are documented publicly on the Security page. Publishing the core library as open source, together with an external audit, is planned before public launch.

Do you track me or use cookies?

No. This website sets no cookies and loads no analytics. The app sends no crash reports or usage data. See the Privacy Policy.
